## 1. Structural change: the report now runs to a reporting date of 30 June 2026

**What changed.** The report previously mixed half-year data with July market data in the same sections. It now carries three explicitly separated dates: a reporting date of 30 June 2026, a publication date in late July 2026, and a positioning period of July to December 2026. Sections 1 to 8 carry only figures as of the reporting date or the source paper's own cut-off between 24 and 26 June. Everything after 30 June has moved into a new Section 9, "Events After the Reporting Date", followed by a new Section 10, "Outlook for H2 2026".

**Why.** Three reasons.

First, this is the convention a professional reader expects. A semi-annual report has a reporting date and a later publication date, and material developments in between are disclosed separately rather than blended into the period record. Publishing a July-dated document that silently stops at 24 June would look negligent, and one that silently mixes July data into the half-year record would be worse.

Second, replicability. The report is rebuilt every half year. A fixed reporting date of 30 June and 31 December makes each edition mechanically comparable and removes the need for the next team to renegotiate where the boundary sits.

Third, it resolves a tension the previous draft carried. The three papers close between 24 and 26 June, so 30 June is the nearest period end and requires no extrapolation, while the July record is genuinely material and now has a place to live.

**Consequence for the next edition.** The H1 2027 Report takes a reporting date of 31 December 2026, with the identical section skeleton. Section 9 then covers January to publication, and Section 10 becomes the outlook for January to June 2027.

## 2. Three corrections from the previous pass have been reversed

The earlier annotated versions of the papers carried reviewer comments flagging these figures as missing or unsupported, so the previous revision removed or qualified them. The final versions contain them, with method. They are restored.

**2.1 Equity portfolio statistics are restored in full.** The blended trailing dividend yield of 1.9%, roughly double the S&P 500's 1.0%, and the betas of 0.86 against MSCI World in EUR and 0.73 against the S&P 500 in EUR now appear in the final equities paper with a complete methodology footnote. The previous revision had corrected this to a 2.9% trough in Q4 2023 on the basis of the reviewer comment. That correction is withdrawn.

**2.3 The duration figures are restored as the paper's own, not as a fund computation.** The final fixed income paper states directly that the 15 to 30 year EUR anchor contributes roughly 62% of the EUR sleeve's duration, and that the EUR sleeve runs approximately 4.6 years, the USD sleeve approximately 3.5 years and the blended book approximately 4.3 years. The previous revision presented these as a fund-level recalculation. That framing has been removed. The paper has also corrected the anchor's effective duration to approximately 15.9 years in its own product table.

## 3. The Volatility Risk Premium review is now in the archive and fully integrated

The paper was absent when the previous revision was written, and every statement resting on it was marked as unverifiable. All of those markers have been removed. Section 7.2 now reports the paper's actual content rather than the department's recollection of it, covering the definition, the four theoretical explanations, the measurement problem, and the five live debates.

Three items from the review are new to the report and are worth the team's attention:

- The **regime-conditioning finding** that the ex-ante premium embedded in VIX futures falls, rather than rises, when ex-ante risk is elevated. This directly contradicts the intuition to add risk into a visible crisis because compensation looks high, and it generalises well beyond options.
- The **tail versus diffusive decomposition**, which establishes that a short volatility position is at its core a short position on disaster risk rather than on diffusive variance.
- The **intermediary channel**, which makes the premium state-dependent on dealer balance-sheet capacity rather than a stable carry.

A new Section 7.3 has been added, binding both reviews to specific decisions in the portfolio work rather than referencing them in the abstract. This addresses the risk that the Research Foundations section reads as decoration.

## 4. Two corrections stand

**4.1 Correlation range.** The commodities paper summarises the cross-correlations in its methodology section as running "approximately 0.33 to 0.34 across the gold, copper and agriculture pairs". The measured matrix and Section 5.4 of the same paper give gold to copper at 0.33, copper to agriculture at 0.34, and gold to agriculture at **0.21**. The report uses the measured range of 0.21 to 0.34. The point is not pedantic. The lowest correlation in the set sits between the sleeve's largest position and its risk dampener, so the shorthand understates the sleeve's diversification. Recommendation: align the methodology shorthand to the measured figures.

**4.2 Valuation discount.** The equities paper quotes the Europe to US valuation gap in three places, as a range of roughly 9% to 26% in the source audit, as roughly 15% to 20% in the synthesis, and as 19.98% in the valuation table. The report uses 19.98%, because it is the only figure derived directly from the two forward multiples and therefore the only one a reader can check.

The commodities paper marks the 863-tonne 2025 central-bank gold purchase figure for confirmation against the World Gold Council's full-year release. ## 6. Two external checks retained on the copper work

Neither invalidates the thesis and both are presented without undermining the team.

**6.1 LME warehouse stocks unit.** The copper dashboard prints 2,111.8 with the unit flagged for verification. Published LME copper stock levels through early July sat in the region of 300,000 to 320,000 tonnes. The figure as printed is a unit error and should be corrected before republication.

**6.2 Refined market balance.** The paper treats a multi-year mine-supply deficit as a structural floor. Published estimates for the refined market point the other way for 2026, with the International Copper Study Group projecting a surplus of roughly 96,000 tonnes for 2026 and 377,000 tonnes for 2027, and Macquarie roughly 262,000 tonnes for 2026. The thesis rests on Chinese physical tightness at a six-month horizon and explicitly treats mine supply as support rather than as the engine, so the recommendation is presentational: frame the structural floor as a long-horizon mine-supply argument rather than as a near-term balance claim.

A third item was added for context, the US Section 232 follow-up report delivered on 30 June recommending a staged tariff of 15% from January 2027 and 30% from January 2028, because it bears directly on the tariff-distortion overlay applied to the inventory signal.

## 7. The oil framework is now the analytical centre of the report

Section 10.2 sets the three gates side by side at the reporting date and at 27 July. The combined signal reads WAIT in both cases, but for opposite reasons. At 30 June the danger was intact and the catalyst had not fired. At 27 July the catalyst has fired decisively while the price has run past the entry band.

Two conclusions are drawn explicitly in the report and both belong in the team's next planning cycle.

The framework worked. It specified the entry condition in advance, in writing, with checkable indicators, and the conditions it named did occur.

A framework is worth little if the decision machinery cannot act inside its window. On the framework's own price bands and the observable catalyst record, there appears to have been a window in the first half of July when Brent traded near $70, inside the high-conviction zone, while the premium was intact and the catalyst had begun to fire. The sleeve was not deployed because the deployment plan begins on approval. **Operational recommendation: a conditional engagement whose window is measured in days needs a standing pre-authorisation and a named decision-maker, not a paper approval cycle.**

## 8. One structural honesty added that is in none of the papers

Section 3.3 states plainly that **the fixed income sleeve is not a hedge for the equity sleeve in an energy-driven shock**. The long-duration positions are built to gain when yields fall in a growth shock. An energy shock raises inflation expectations and yields, so the bond anchor loses money at the same time as the reflation trades gain. The July record confirms this.

No single team could have seen it, because each built its sleeve independently. It is exactly the kind of statement an external partner expects a fund-level document to make, and omitting it would leave a reader with unearned comfort about the three-sleeve structure.
